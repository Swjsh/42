## The load-bearing finding: the ledger's own `drift_detected` flag

**All 23 ledger rows carry `drift_detected: true`.** In every row the anchor
PRICE is byte-identical between the canonical (5m) and `alt_points_res15` (15m)
capture, but the anchor TIME differs -- by an amount that varies row to row
(65400s for `5EWHJK`, 34800s for `S3pkoW`, 235200s for `dk6RTY`, ...), i.e. not a
fixed clock-skew constant correctable with one offset. This means J's own
capture pipeline already knows anchor timing is not reliable to single-bar
precision for these 23 rows -- this is a pre-existing data-quality fact, not
something this session introduced.

Given that, the validation harness tries BOTH the canonical and the alt15
anchor pair for each line (never a third, invented interpretation) and accepts
either. This widens the search; it does not loosen the price or slope
tolerance. Even with this widening: 0/24.

**The dominant story is the "close but just outside" class, not "nowhere

candidate explanation** for the 0% pass rate; it is Opus's call (WS-F.3) whether
to widen the constant, not something this session tuned to force a pass.

The 5 "far off" and several of the slope mismatches trace to the SAME root

different slopes from the identical price pair (confirmed for `dk6RTY`:
canonical slope 0.073/bar vs the fitted candidate's -0.045/bar, a sign flip --
the two plausible time interpretations bracket genuinely different geometries).
