## Q1 — Sizing: what would equity-scaled sizing have produced?

Every arm today traded a flat clamped qty (3, 5, or 10 — fleet arms hit a `recency RED` qty
clamp; risky-3's `qty10 <$0.50 boost` overrode its own clamp back up because both its entries
were sub-$0.50 premium). None of the 14 trades came close to CLAUDE.md Rule 6's actual
per-trade risk cap (Gamma-Safe 30% of equity, Gamma-Bold/fleet-risky 50%) — realized
`%equity` ranged 3.2%–14.0%, the cap is 30-50%.

**Counterfactual: what if each arm sized every trade today to its own doctrine cap** (same
entry/exit prices, quantity scaled to `floor(equity × cap ÷ (entry_px × 100))`):

| Wave | Actual $ | At-cap $ | Multiple |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wave 1 (loser, all 5) | **−303** | **−1,492** | 4.9x worse |
| Wave 2 (winner, 4 arms) | **+611** | **+2,063** | 3.4x better |
| Wave 3 (scratch, 4 arms) | 0 | −2 | noise |
| safe-2 bollinger put (loser) | −42 | −224 | 5.3x worse |
| **Day total** | **+266** | **+345** | **1.3x better, net** |

Worked example on the single worst trade (bold-2, 10:41, −$105 actual): at its 50% cap
(equity $5,048 → $2,524 notional → 22 contracts vs. the 5 actually bought), the identical
−18.75% move would have cost **−$462** — **$357 more lost on that one trade alone.**

**Honest both-directions read:** at-cap sizing turns a good day marginally better in
aggregate (+$345 vs +$266) but does so by multiplying the variance ~4-5x in both directions.
On a day where wave 1 (the loser) came FIRST, an arm sized to cap would have opened −$1,492
before the winning wave even fired — well inside Rule 5's kill-switch band at the smaller
account sizes (Gamma-Safe-2 $5,501: −$1,492 = −27% of day-start equity, one wave away from
tripping the −30% kill switch on a SINGLE bad wave). Some of the theoretical cap sizes
(risky-3 at 70-76 contracts on a sub-$0.35 0DTE OTM print) are not realistically fillable at
that size without material slippage this analysis does not model. **Not a recommendation —
presented as a pre-registered hypothesis below.**

> **PRE-REG: equity-scaled-to-Rule-6-cap sizing** — kill criterion: if measured on ≥15
> independent (not same-wave-correlated) trading days the at-cap simulation's worst single-day
> drawdown exceeds the kill-switch band more than once, OR modeled slippage at scaled size
> erases >25% of the aggregate uplift, kill it. Not shippable off n=1 day.

## Q3 — Positioning: was the 10:41 stop noise or a broken thesis?

**The impulse, verified on real 1-min SPY bars (not the −$2.07 headline number — re-derived,
matches exactly):** SPY closed the 14:25Z (10:25 ET) bar at **769.26**, then printed a high of
**771.33** at 14:40Z (10:40 ET) — a $2.07 move in 15 minutes. All 5 arms entered 771C/773C
during the 14:41Z bar (10:41 ET), i.e. **one minute after the impulse's high print**, at an
average fill SPY of ~771.14-771.33.

**The stop:** structure_stop was set at 770.85 (the level that had just been reclaimed —
`INTRADAY_RTH_HIGH_2026-08-19`). SPY's first 1-min bar to **close** below that level was
14:49Z (10:49 ET, close 770.76) — not a wick, a confirmed bar close, 8 minutes after entry.
Exits filled ~10:51-10:52. **Adverse move at the confirmed break: 771.14 → 770.76 = −0.049%
(−$0.38), matching J's "−0.05%" almost exactly.**

**What happened next is the load-bearing fact:** SPY did NOT continue down. It reversed
inside the same 14:49-14:52Z window and by 14:59Z (10:59 ET) printed **772.20** — a new
session high, +$1.44 above the level that had just stopped the trade out. The 771C contract
sold at 0.91-0.97 traded back up to **$1.93** by 15:01Z (11:01 ET, +~100% from the stop-out
exits). **This was a shaken-out shallow pullback inside a still-intact uptrend, not a broken
thesis** — the direction was right, the entry was simply made at the exact local top of a
15-minute impulse with no pullback margin, so a retracement worth less than a nickel on SPY
was enough to trigger a structural exit before the move resumed.

## Limitations (read before acting on anything above)

- **n=14 trades, ONE calendar day.** Every number in this document is a same-day
  observation, not a backtested or walk-forward-validated result. Nothing here clears the
  OOS/WF/sub-window bar in CLAUDE.md rule 11.
- **These are not 14 independent observations.** 13 of 14 are the same
  `BULLISH_RECLAIM_RIDE_THE_RIBBON` signal from `build_shared_signal.py`, sampled by up to 5
  correlated arms across 3 distinct signal moments (r=0.846 book-wide per prior sessions).
  Effectively **3 independent entry decisions**, replayed 4-5 ways each by sizing/gate
  variant. Any "3 of 5 arms won" framing is describing one coin flip observed from 5 seats,
  not 5 flips.
- **The strike ladder (Q2) uses same-bar OPRA prints for a fair same-moment comparison** —
  it is real, not simulated — but it is a single day's single signal moment. Do not
  generalize "ITM beats OTM in dollars" beyond this one 0DTE ribbon-ride instance without
  more evidence.
- **The sizing counterfactual (Q1) is arithmetic on real fills at hypothetical quantities** —
  it does not model slippage, fill quality, or liquidity impact at 5-15x the actual size
  (some hypothetical quantities, e.g. risky-3 at 70-76 OTM 0DTE contracts, are unrealistic to
  fill at the quoted price). Treat the dollar figures as directional, not exact.
- **No params change is recommended anywhere in this document.** The sizing counterfactual is
  written as a pre-registered hypothesis with a named kill criterion specifically so it is
  not read as a ship-it. Per OP-16/CLAUDE.md rule 11, shipping requires OOS positive + WF≥0.70
  + sub-window stable + anchor no-regression + an A/B scorecard filed at
  `analysis/recommendations/{rule_id}.json` — none of which exist yet for anything above.
- **The 12-vs-14 round-trip discrepancy** is corrected here from source data
  (`journal/trades.csv`, cross-footed to the penny against the $266 book total) — flagged
  per this task's own instruction to re-derive rather than trust the prior session's count.
- **risky-3's exit mechanism at 10:49 (`premium_stop@0.26`)** was not independently traced
  through `exit_manager.py`'s per-arm config in this session — flagged as unresolved rather
  than guessed at.
