## 5. Findings that need action

### 🚨 F1 — the bear path was structurally shut, and only one detector could open it

**Filter 8 on the bear side requires `VIX > 17.30 AND rising`.** VIX traded 15.49–16.13 all session. It was therefore **not "close" — it was unpassable**, on every tick, by construction. My earlier framing ("one small block away") was wrong, and so was the intuitive reading of the ledger: a bear score of 9 with only filter 8 outstanding looks like a near-miss but is actually a hard stop.

**So how did anything trade at all?** `filters.py` carries a *trendline-only bypass*: when `trendline_rejection` is the **SOLE** level-tied trigger, blockers **5, 8 and 9 are removed** and become −1 score demerits each. That bypass was the only road into a bear entry today, and it opened exactly when the trendline shape formed — 12:56.

**The inverted priority, in today's data:**

| Trigger (raw detections) | Times fired | Made it into effective triggers |
|---|---|---|
| `level_rejection` | **108** | **0** |
| `confluence` | **67** | **0** |
| `trendline_rejection` | 75 | **40** |

The *stronger* triggers fired 175 times between them and converted **zero** times — because they get the FULL filter set, including the unpassable VIX gate. The *weakest* trigger got the free pass. **That is the exact asymmetry the G2 pre-registration was written to test**, and `filters.py` itself records that "89% of all bear ENTER verdicts over 33 sessions came through this bypass alone."

**Status:** the G2 A/B already ran 2026-08-01 and returned NEITHER_SHIPS — but ARM_EXTEND's primary gate was **UNDETERMINED, not FAIL**, because 3 of the 25 recent days had zero cached OPRA contracts. Re-running on data through 2026-08-20 (20 new trading days, partial OPRA backfill) is queued as the follow-up. **No threshold is being changed on the strength of one good day.**

### ⚠️ F2 — bold-2 was blocked 16 times by `require_bearish_fill_bar`

At 12:56–13:12 safe entered and **bold was gated by an entry-gate flag safe does not have**, so it entered 20 minutes later at a worse basis (0.34 vs safe's 0.70 on a different strike, but the same move). ### 📋 F4 — `trendlines.json` is empty and is NOT the trigger source

Worth stating plainly because the names collide: every entry today triggered on `trendline_rejection`, but that comes from **`detect_trendline_rejection_bearish` in `filters.py`**, which fits a line through the last 3 decreasing 5m pivot highs **live, in memory**. It never reads `automation/state/trendlines.json` — that file (count: 0, manual 0, auto 0) is a separate shadow artifact with no consumers. **The trigger is real; the file is unrelated.**

### ✅ F5 — WITHDRAWN. The "112c spread" finding was my misreading

The original note flagged a median **112.9c** spread on every tick and asked why filter 6 never blocked. **That was wrong twice over.** `spread_cents` in the decisions ledger is the **Saty ribbon spread** (fast-to-slow distance), not an option bid-ask. And filter 6 blocks when `spread_cents < RIBBON_SPREAD_MIN_CENTS` — it requires a spread of **at least** 30c, i.e. it blocks a *compressed* ribbon, not a wide one.

So a 112c median ribbon spread means the ribbon was **strongly stretched all day** — a clean trend, exactly the condition filter 6 exists to confirm. It passed 386/386 because it should have. Nothing to fix; the finding is retracted.
