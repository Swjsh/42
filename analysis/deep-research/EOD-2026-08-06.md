*ORACLE = perfect exit at the 1m session high after entry (2.80 @ 12:16). **ORACLE is not

"best 1m close" is real: its market sell filled *inside* the 12:16 bar above that bar's close.

### The mechanism: a snapshot bid, polled once a minute

The exit manager fires TP1 when its **own** once-per-minute `best_premium` snapshot crosses the level.
safe-2 and risky-3 are **byte-identical on this dimension** — same entry (1.28), same
`tp1_premium_pct=1.0`, same TP1 level **2.56**. They polled the same contract in the same minute and
saw different quotes:

| poll | safe-2 sees | risky-3 sees | Δ | outcome |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 12:01 | 2.43 | 2.43 | 0.00 | neither fires |
| 12:03 | 2.27 | 2.29 | −0.02 | neither fires |
| **12:04** | **2.53** | **2.56** | **−0.03** | **risky-3 TP1 fires; safe-2 misses by 3¢** |
| 12:16 | **2.70** | (already out) | — | safe-2 TP1 fires, fills 2.71 |

Across **98 shared polls, 82 disagreed by ≥1¢** (max 9¢). The 12:04 divergence straddled the trigger.

### CONFIGURATION vs QUOTE LUCK — the decomposition

Using each arm's **real logged quote stream** and the other arm's **realized fills** as the
counterfactual outcome (no modeling of quotes):

| counterfactual | result | delta vs actual |
|---|---:|---:|
| safe-2 had fired at 12:04 (like risky-3) | $105.67/ct → $317.00 | **−$58.00** |
| risky-3 had missed 12:04 and fired 12:16 (like safe-2) | $122.75/ct → $982.00 | **+$152.00** |
| **total day P&L riding on that 3-cent divergence** | | **$210.00** |

| pair | driver | size |
|---|---|---:|
| **safe-2 vs risky-3** | **100% QUOTE LUCK** — identical TP1 config | $21.25/ct ($210 book-wide) |
| **risky-1 vs the others** | **CONFIGURATION** — `exit_patch` TP1 at +50% vs +100% | $67.20/ct |

Neutralize the config and risky-1 lands on top of safe-2: under +100% TP1 on its own entry (1.23 →
level 2.46, first crossed 12:04), risky-1 makes **$126.40/ct vs safe-2's $125.00/ct**, i.e.
**+$336.00** instead of +$296.00. *(Counterfactual: uses safe-2's logged stream after 11:29 — the same
contract — since risky-1 was flat by then. Labeled, n=1.)*

**Answer to the question asked: yes, the TP1-level A/B is underpowered.** On a single trade, pure
quote luck moved **$210** while the config effect it is trying to measure was **$336** — luck is
**62%** of signal (per-contract: $21.25 vs $67.20, **32%**). Same shape as the 08-04 finding where
risky-1 gained $57 of $347 on a half-cent TP1 miss. **Two arms with identical config are a
noise-floor instrument, not a redundancy** — that is the useful thing they produced today. Do not
ratify a TP1-level change on anything close to this n.
