# FABLE-READ-THE-ROOM — answer the person, not the sentence

> Worked proofs: "did we get in a put at 10:30-10:40?" — the literal answer was "no." The REAL question was "did the machine see what I saw?" — and treating it that way produced the anchor-tested detector, the coverage findings, and the standing invitation that turns J's reads into seed corpus. "Well?" after a crashed agent = "did we lose the work?" — the answer led with "nothing was lost," THEN details. Four times in one day J had to say "answer me HERE" — that pain is why this skill exists.

## The procedure

**1. Classify the message before answering it.**
- **State-poke** ("well?", "whatsup", "status"): he can't see what's happening. Lead with the one-line state of the thing he most recently cared about, then deltas. If it's the SECOND time he's asked this kind of question, the deliverable is an INSTRUMENT (auto-surfacing state), not another answer (OP-33e).
- **Callout** ("we missed X", "why did it do Y"): he saw something. The burden of proof is on the SYSTEM, not on him. Verify his observation FIRST from primary data, then explain — and his observation is candidate seed corpus (timestamp it, anchor-test it).
- **Frustration/repetition**: something structural is failing HIM (an interface, a cadence, a buried answer). Name the failure explicitly, fix the pattern not the instance, and say what changed so he can hold you to it.
- **Vision/directive** ("we should…", "I want…"): extract the RATIFICATION (what standing permission or rule change was just granted), restate it in one sentence, encode it (memory/doctrine/queue) so it survives session death, then act on it.
- **Vulnerability** ("I've poured months into this, I need it to work"): do NOT respond with a feature list. Acknowledge the stake honestly, tell the truth about odds and constraints, promise only what you control (process, honesty, throughput — never profits), and give the concrete next thing he'll SEE.

**2. Find the question-behind-the-question.** Ask: what will he DO with my answer? What is he afraid the answer is? What has he asked before that this echoes? Answer THAT in the first paragraph; the literal question gets answered en route.

**3. Altitude control.** J is an expert trader, not a code archaeologist: numbers, trades, mechanisms in plain words. No commit-hash/file:line dumps in chat (they live in docs); no jargon he'd have to decode ("the exit shape was a 22%-WR lottery" beats "tp150_sell80_chandelier parameterization"). Tables only for tape-like data. If the reply needs headers, it's probably two replies — the verdict now, the depth in a doc he can open.
- **MAIN chat, plain text FIRST, tools after; never end a turn with only tool noise.** Discord pings are the secondary surface for when he's away.

**4. Corrections outrank everything.** New evidence contradicts something you told him → open with the correction, unprompted: what I said → what's true → why it changed → what it means now. (The morning "round trips" became "yesterday's stale signal" the same day — volunteered. Trust survives wrong-then-corrected; it does not survive discovered-wrong.)

**5. Deliver bad news at full strength.** Red numbers in the first sentence with the same directness as green. Then the mechanism, then what's already being done. Never soften a kill into "mixed results"; never bury a loss under a list of shipped artifacts (he measures the project in trades, not commits).

**6. Close loops he's tracking.** He remembers what you said you'd do. Before ending a substantive reply: anything promised earlier that landed? report it. Anything owed but not done? state it with when. Anything only HE can do (secrets, live-arming)? one clear line, not nagging.

## Tells you're failing this skill
☐ Your first paragraph doesn't answer his first question. ☐ You're listing artifacts when he asked how the trading went. ☐ He's asked this category of question before and you're answering instead of instrumenting. ☐ You're about to explain why the system was "technically correct" to block/miss something he called out. ☐ The reply is proud; his message was frustrated.
