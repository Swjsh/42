## VERDICT

**Yes, J's "PDT went away" claim is TRUE, but only half the picture — and there's a live contradiction in Gamma's own setup worth knowing.**

- **The $25,000 pattern-day-trader minimum for MARGIN accounts is gone.** FINRA eliminated day-trade counting and the $25k floor via a Rule 4210 amendment (**SR-FINRA-2025-017**, SEC-approved **2026-04-14**, effective **2026-06-04**), replacing it with real-time/once-daily "intraday margin deficit" monitoring. No more PDT designation, no more 4-trades-in-5-days trigger. Standard Reg-T margin minimum ($2,000) is unchanged and is now the only account-size floor.
- **Cash accounts were NEVER subject to PDT** — that part of J's mental model needed no updating. What gates a cash account is settlement (T+1) + Good Faith Violations, unchanged, detailed in the linked 07-14 doc.
- **Options, including 0DTE, are explicitly named** in the rule's own record as a motivating gap the old framework missed — the new rule covers them.
- **The catch: an 18-month firm-level implementation grace window runs through 2027-10-20**, and Alpaca's own Customer Agreement explicitly reserves the right to keep applying legacy PDT to *specific accounts* during that window even though Alpaca says it flipped the switch firm-wide on 2026-06-04. **Not independently confirmed against Gamma's actual Safe-2/Bold-2 accounts** — do that check before treating this as settled for our own book.
- **Separate, unrelated finding that IS directly relevant to Gamma's architecture:** Alpaca does not offer true cash brokerage accounts at all — confirmed 2026-08-18, "all accounts are set up as margin accounts." This conflicts with the "Gamma-Safe-2 is a cash account" premise `pdt_gate_mode=cash_settlement` is built on (already separately flagged as stale in `PDT-ACCOUNT-TYPE-DECISION-2026-08-06.md`). This doc doesn't resolve that — it's evidence for J's pending decision there.

**What was eliminated, verbatim from FINRA's own notice** (fetched directly): the notice "replaced 'day trade count requirements for designating a customer as a pattern day trader' and 'the $25,000 pattern day trader minimum equity requirement,'" striking paragraph (f)(8)(B) and related provisions under (b), (f)(10), (g)(13) of Rule 4210. — [FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10), fetched 2026-08-18.

**What replaced it:** an "**intraday margin deficit**" (IML) standard. FINRA's own investor page, fetched directly: *"If an imbalance occurs and your account doesn't have enough equity to cover your open positions, you have an 'intraday margin deficit' that you're expected to satisfy as promptly as possible."* Firms may run real-time monitoring (block the deficit-creating trade) **or** a once-daily EOD calculation + margin call — real-time is permitted, not mandated. — [FINRA: Understanding the New Intraday Margin Requirements](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements), fetched directly 2026-08-18.

**Scope — margin accounts only, cash accounts untouched.** FINRA Reg Notice 26-10 applies to "each margin account of a customer... other than a good faith account or portfolio margin account" — cash accounts aren't mentioned because they were never in scope to begin with (unchanged from the 07-14 doc's finding). Portfolio margin accounts get a related-but-separate amendment (below, Q5).

**Does the SEC's OWN investor-facing page reflect this yet?** Partially, and this is worth knowing — **investor.gov's PDT glossary page**, fetched directly 2026-08-18, still leads with the *old* definition ("must have at least $25,000 in their accounts") in its primary definition text, and only acknowledges the change in a secondary note about the intraday-margin replacement — it does not clearly state the $25k floor is gone. **This is a live SEC-hosted page giving an outdated primary impression as of today** — flagged explicitly in Unverified/Conflicting below. Don't hand J's mental model back to that specific page without the caveat.

**What counts as a "day trade" now — did the definition itself change?** No — the *text* of "day trade" is unchanged: "the purchase and sale, or the sale and purchase, of the same security on the same day in a margin account," confirmed independently by tastytrade's own current page ("the opening and closing of the same position within the same trading session," for stocks, ETFs, **and options**) — [tastytrade.com/learn/markets/industry/pattern-day-trading/](https://tastytrade.com/learn/markets/industry/pattern-day-trading/), fetched directly 2026-08-18, page last-updated 2026-06-11. What changed is that this definition no longer *triggers* a threshold-based restriction (the 4-in-5-day count + $25k floor) — it's now an input to the intraday-margin-deficit calculation instead of a classification gate.

**Is the 4-in-5-business-days trigger itself gone?** **Yes, as an operative restriction.** No source found — FINRA's own, any broker's, or any law-firm client alert — describes a still-live 4-in-5 count anywhere in the new framework. The PDT *designation* itself is abolished; the phrase "pattern day trader" is a historical label going forward, not a status an account can trigger into.

## Q2 — What replaced it, and the cash-account fallback

**New mechanism, precisely:** intraday margin deficit (IML) monitoring — not a new fixed dollar floor, a continuous solvency check. The **$2,000 figure** that keeps appearing is **not new** — it's the pre-existing standard Reg-T minimum equity to open/use a margin account at all (unchanged, decades old), which is now the *only* equity floor left once the PDT-specific $25k requirement is gone. Confirmed directly on FINRA's own page: *"There's no $25,000 minimum equity requirement for day trading... [but] $2,000 remains the minimum to engage in leveraged trading generally."* — [FINRA: Understanding the New Intraday Margin Requirements](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements), fetched directly 2026-08-18.

**The transition-period nuance that matters most for picking/trusting a broker right now:** FINRA's rule gives *member firms* up to 18 months (to 2027-10-20) to fully implement. Alpaca's own docs describe this internally as **"a 12-month interim transition period... during which member firms may apply either the legacy PDT rules or the new intraday margin rules (by account or across the firm) as they transition their systems"** — [docs.alpaca.markets: Understanding FINRA's New Intraday Margin Rule and the End of PDT](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/understanding-finras-new-intraday-margin-rule-and-the-end-of-pdt), fetched directly 2026-08-18. Alpaca's "12-month" framing doesn't state an exact end date and isn't obviously the same window as FINRA's 18-month/Oct-2027 firm deadline — treat FINRA's own text as the authoritative outer bound, and confirm any specific broker's *own* cutover status directly (see Q4 — Alpaca and tastytrade both say they went live 2026-06-04; nobody should assume every broker did).

**Cash-account path — unchanged, still fully valid, not re-derived here.** Full mechanics (T+1 settlement, Good Faith Violations, the 3-GFV/90-day restriction, freeriding under Reg T) are already documented in [`CASH-ACCOUNT-DAY-TRADING-REGULATIONS-2026-07-14.md`](../research/CASH-ACCOUNT-DAY-TRADING-REGULATIONS-2026-07-14.md) — nothing found today changes any of that; it predates and is structurally independent of the PDT/margin rule change covered above. Bottom line repeated from that doc: a cash account can day-trade freely on *settled* funds; the friction is GFVs from reusing *unsettled* T+1 proceeds for a second/third same-day round trip, not a trade-count ceiling.

## Q3 — Options-specific: does this apply the same way, and what about 0DTE?

**Yes, options are covered identically to equities — and 0DTE is explicitly named in the rulemaking record as a gap the OLD rule missed.** From FINRA's own investor page, fetched directly: *"[the new standard] will typically cover all activity in your margin account during the day, including margin used for trading zero-day-to-expiration (0DTE) options."* — [FINRA: Understanding the New Intraday Margin Requirements](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements), fetched 2026-08-18.

From the original rule-change filing notice (Federal Register, via search synthesis of the SEC/FINRA filing text): *"The expansion in 0DTE options trading, which generally does not qualify as day trading under the current rule, has emerged as a new risk not covered by current rule."* — [Federal Register notice of SR-FINRA-2025-017](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/14/2026-00519/self-regulatory-organizations-financial-industry-regulatory-authority-inc-notice-of-filing-of-a), via search 2026-08-18 (also independently fetched directly in the 2026-07-14 predecessor doc). Alpaca's own docs page states the mechanism directly: *"The expiration of a long option is explicitly classified as an IML-reducing transaction, ensuring these instruments are properly margined."* — [docs.alpaca.markets](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/understanding-finras-new-intraday-margin-rule-and-the-end-of-pdt), fetched directly 2026-08-18.

**Options exercise/assignment same-day treatment:** the rule text permits members to treat "the substantially contemporaneous execution of multiple legs of a spread, or the creation of a position by the assignment or exercise of an option and the liquidation of such position during the same day" as occurring simultaneously — i.e., an assignment-then-same-day-close doesn't necessarily get double-counted as two separate day-trade legs. Confirmed independently both by direct fetch of FINRA Reg Notice 26-10 and by search synthesis of the underlying SEC filing text. — fetched/searched 2026-08-18.

**No new OCC/exchange-level 0DTE-specific position limits, trading halts, or product restrictions were found.** This is a margin-*methodology* change, not a product ban or a new position-limit regime. An SEC roundtable did surface panelist concern that PDT removal could *increase* 0DTE-driven retail account "churn" — noted as industry commentary, not a rule: *"panelists cautioned that 0DTE creates 'a lot more churn on retail accounts,' and that removing the pattern day trader rule would amplify this further."* — via search of [Katten roundtable writeup](https://quickreads.ext.katten.com/post/102mqqs/navigating-the-future-of-us-options-markets-takeaways-from-the-sec-roundtable), 2026-08-18. A separate SEC DERA staff paper titled "Hope at a Reasonable Price: Customer Use of Limit Orders in the 0DTE Market" exists and appears squarely on-topic, but sec.gov returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch (twice) — **could not verify its contents, not cited for any specific finding.**

## Q5 — Everything else J didn't ask about but should know

### Tax treatment: SPY vs SPX/XSP is a real, structural, non-trivial difference

**SPX (and its mini, XSP) options qualify as Section 1256 "nonequity options"; SPY options do not — confirmed by IRS Form 6781's own framework** (the PDF's own text could not be extracted verbatim by the fetch tool — flagged below — but multiple independent secondary tax sources describe the same IRS framework consistently): a "nonequity option" under IRC §1256(g) includes broad-based stock index options; SPX and XSP are cash-settled, broad-based-index options and qualify. SPY is an option **on an ETF share** (a security), not directly on the index — so it's an "equity option" and does **not** qualify, even though SPY tracks the same S&P 500 exposure. — synthesized from [TurboTax: What is Form 6781](https://turbotax.intuit.com/tax-tips/investments-and-taxes/what-is-form-6781-gains-and-losses-from-section-1256-contracts-and-straddles/L2rfcJXT9), [Schwab Advisor Services: How Are Options Taxed](https://advisorservices.schwab.com/content/how-are-options-taxed), and specialist options-tax explainers, all via search 2026-08-18; IRS Form 6781 itself located at [irs.gov/pub/irs-access/f6781_accessible.pdf](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-access/f6781_accessible.pdf) but its encoded PDF text was **not directly extractable** by this session's fetch tool.

**What this is worth:** Section 1256 gains get a blended **60% long-term / 40% short-term** rate *regardless of actual holding period* — relevant for 0DTE, which by definition never holds long enough to earn long-term treatment any other way. One source's worked example: a top-bracket trader nets roughly **26.8%** blended effective rate under 1256 vs up to **37-40.8%** ordinary short-term rate on economically-identical SPY exposure — via search synthesis, not independently recomputed here, treat as illustrative not exact. Section 1256 contracts are also **mark-to-market at year-end** (open positions deemed sold 12/31, new basis set) and are **exempt from the wash-sale rule** — both confirmed independently across multiple sources. SPY options get neither: ordinary short-term-almost-always capital gains AND the wash-sale rule applies.

**This is currently moot for Gamma (paper trading, no tax event) but becomes a real go-live decision, not a rules-engine detail.**

### Assignment / exercise risk: SPY (physical) vs SPX/XSP (cash) — and a live Alpaca development that changes the calculus

- **SPY**: American-style, physically-settled. Stops trading 4:00pm ET; assignment delivers/removes actual 100 shares per contract at the strike. **Pin risk is real**: if the closing print lands very close to the strike, ITM/OTM status can be ambiguous until settlement, creating possible unwanted overnight share exposure and gap risk before the next session. — synthesized from [OptionAlpha: 0DTE Partial ITM Assignment Pin Risk](https://optionalpha.com/learn/0dte-partial-in-the-money-assignment) and several SPX-vs-SPY comparison sites, via search 2026-08-18.
- **SPX / XSP**: European-style (exercise only possible at expiration — no early assignment ever), cash-settled (a credit/debit, never shares). The 0DTE-tradable SPX series is specifically **SPXW** (PM-settled, same-day expirations, stops trading 4:15pm ET) — **not** the classic monthly SPX ticker, which is AM-settled and stops trading the *day before* expiration and is therefore not usable for a same-day 0DTE trade at all. This AM/PM distinction is a common source of confusion and is worth stating precisely: *"When traders say 'SPX 0DTE,' the contracts are almost always SPXW series."* — synthesized from multiple sources incl. [MarketData.app: SPX vs SPXW](https://www.marketdata.app/education/options/spx-vs-spxw-options/), via search 2026-08-18. XSP carries the same European/cash-settled/1256 profile at 1/10th SPX's notional (roughly SPY-comparable dollar size) — [Cboe XSP fact sheet](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/xsp/XSP_Options_Fact_Sheet.pdf), located via search 2026-08-18 (PDF, not independently text-extracted — see Unverified).
- **Live, dated development directly relevant here**: Alpaca added **paper-trading** support for index options — **SPX, SPXW, VIX, VIXW, DJX, XSP** — on **2026-07-23**, explicitly marketed around removing assignment risk: *"Test... expiry logic without early-assignment paths."* — [alpaca.markets/blog/alpaca-introduces-index-options-paper-trading/](https://alpaca.markets/blog/alpaca-introduces-index-options-paper-trading/), fetched directly 2026-08-18, published 2026-07-23. **This is paper-only — no committed live-trading date found anywhere.** Also: Alpaca's own generic "Will we offer index options?" support FAQ page still says (stale, dated content from ~March 2025) that Alpaca doesn't offer index options at all, for either paper or live — a direct, unresolved contradiction with the July 2026 blog post on Alpaca's own site. Treat the July 2026 blog post as current and the FAQ page as unmaintained; treat "when does live SPX/XSP trading arrive at Alpaca" as genuinely unknown.

### Options Regulatory Fee (ORF) — exchanges revised rates mid-2026, methodology changed July 1

A new **assessment methodology** took effect **2026-07-01**: ORF is now assessed only on options transactions actually cleared in the "Customer" range at OCC (narrower base than before). Several exchanges revised their per-contract-side rate at the same time — e.g., Nasdaq ISE $0.0092→$0.0080, Nasdaq MRX $0.0116→$0.0080, Nasdaq GEMX $0.0116→$0.0100, Nasdaq Texas $0.0198→$0.0200 (increase), NYSE Arca $0.0120 (through 2026-08-31) → $0.0080 (from 2026-09-01). — synthesized from multiple Federal Register SRO filing notices (Cboe EDGX, NYSE Arca, Nasdaq ISE/MRX/GEMX/Texas), via search 2026-08-18; not independently fetched line-by-line. These are exchange-assessed, broker-passed-through fees, layered on top of whatever the broker itself charges (see Q4) — at 0DTE's typical sub-$1 premiums, cents-level pass-throughs are a real (if small) drag worth knowing exists, not worth over-modeling at Gamma's current paper-only stage.

### Portfolio margin — not relevant at Gamma's scale, noted for completeness

Portfolio margin requires roughly **$125,000 initial / $100,000 maintenance** equity (Schwab's stated figures) — an order of magnitude above Gamma's ~$5.5k account, irrelevant for now. FINRA's 2026 amendments also touched portfolio-margin accounts (requiring firms with <$5M in portfolio-margin equity to hold intraday margin "substantially similar" to EOD margin) but did **not** change the $125k/$100k entry threshold itself. — [Schwab: Portfolio Margin](https://www.schwab.com/margin/portfolio-margin), via search 2026-08-18.

### 0DTE regulatory temperature check

No outright 0DTE ban, position limit, or SPY/SPX-specific restriction found anywhere, as of 2026-08-18 (consistent with the 2026-07-14 predecessor doc's finding, unchanged since). 0DTE is explicitly named in FINRA's own rulemaking record as a risk category the old framework missed and the new one addresses (Q3, above) — regulatory attention is active but expressed through the margin-methodology change already covered, not a separate crackdown. One SEC roundtable panelist flagged that removing PDT could increase 0DTE-driven retail churn (Q3) — commentary, not policy.
