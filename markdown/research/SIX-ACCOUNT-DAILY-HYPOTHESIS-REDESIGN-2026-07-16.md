## 3. THE REDESIGN — per-account daily-hypothesis architecture

### 3.1 What the old "arms = risk profiles" doctrine protected, and why it can't deliver 6 daily hypotheses

The doctrine (`fleet/accounts.json` `_doc`) held arms differ **only** by sizing/gates on one shared signal (`build_shared_signal.py`), explicitly to isolate the effect of a sizing/gate change in a clean A/B. That is a legitimate, evidence-grounded design for answering "does tighter/looser gating help *this* signal" — and the forensic data proves it worked exactly as designed: safe-3 and risky-1 post **identical** enter-counts on every single day of the window. But per Grinold's Fundamental Law (Information Ratio ∝ IC × √Breadth) and the finance-literature finding that same-signal-different-leverage strategies correlate ≈1.0 (external evidence, moderate–strong confidence), three arms on one signal contribute **effectively zero incremental breadth** — they are one bet sized three ways, not three chances to find what works. That is the literal, evidence-backed indictment of why the fleet has never been able to answer "which setup works" faster by having 3 arms instead of 1: it was never testing 3 setups.

**J's pivot is correct on the mechanism.** The fix is not more gate tuning on the shared signal — it's giving each account a **distinct signal generator** wherever one is validated or near-validated, and preserving the two things the old doctrine actually protected:

1. **Validation cleanliness** — each new per-account hypothesis still needs its own eval-first A/B scorecard (OP-11) before scaling, exactly as gap_and_go and J_VWAP_RECLAIM_FB already have. Running 6 concurrent hypotheses is running 6 simultaneous trials — per the Deflated Sharpe Ratio literature (Bailey & López de Prado, strong confidence), the false-positive bar must rise with the number of concurrent trials being evaluated. **Tighten the evidence_n floor from ≥15 to ≥30** (practitioner consensus floor, moderate confidence) for any hypothesis that only became live because of this 6-way pivot, and treat the existing OP-11 gates (OOS positive, WF ≥0.70, sub-window-stable, anchor no-regression) as a necessary-not-sufficient bar under multi-testing.
2. **Risk isolation** — Rule 5's per-account kill switches (Safe -30%/day, Bold -50%/day) already isolate blowup risk per account; this survives the pivot unchanged. Worth flagging (not mandating): the closest real-world analog to 6-pods-each-with-its-own-hypothesis — multi-manager platforms (Millennium/Citadel/Point72) — reportedly run pod-level drawdown cuts around 5%/7.5%, materially tighter than Gamma's 30%/50% (external evidence, moderate confidence, figures not traced to primary disclosure). Flagged as a genuine "no obvious right answer" fork for J, not shipped unilaterally.

## 4. THE 30%-SCALP QUESTION, WITH NUMBERS

**J's literal ask (a genuine 30% TP, multiple times a day, 5-15 min holds) has never been tested on our own data and no external dataset supports it as a structurally sound long-premium shape either.**

**Ours:**
- Every backtest grid this codebase has ever run for a scalp-shaped exit tested `tp_premium_pct ∈ {0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0}` — **30% was never in the grid**, min tested is 50%.
- The one adversarially friction-swept shape we have (`shotgun_scalper` ratified combo: TP+150%/stop-35%/OTM-2/12-min/qty3) is **linear-dollar** friction-sensitive: round-trip cost = 6× cents-per-side at qty3. At the codebase's own **standing default** friction (2¢/side, hardcoded in `simulator_real.py:107-108` and `simulator_credit.py:69-71` — not a stress scenario, the default), this combo's expectancy collapses from $15.22/tr (frictionless) to **$3.91/tr**, Sharpe 4.50→1.14, and **4 of 7 ratification gates flip to FAIL.** At 3¢/side it goes **negative** (-$2.10/tr).
- Because friction is fixed-dollar, not %, it eats a *larger share* of a *smaller* TP's thinner per-winner dollar gain. The closest tested analog to J's ask (TP=50%, OTM-1, matching the original playbook design) has frictionless expectancy $14.55/tr but has **never been friction-swept**; applying the same fixed-dollar formula as an estimate puts it at roughly **~$2.55/tr post-friction** — thinner than even the marginal 150% shape.
- **Conclusion from our own data: no tested shape clears zero expectancy at a genuine 30% TP, and the mechanism says a 30% TP would be structurally worse, not better, than the shapes we've already found marginal.**

**External:** SEC DERA's 0DTE limit-order study (SPXW, not SPY — coarser tick, magnitudes not directly portable) puts non-marketable-limit round-trip cost around $0.04-0.07 on sub-$3 contracts; back-of-envelope, a 30% TP needs roughly **$0.35-1.00 entry premium** to clear that friction comfortably via passive limit, or **$0.33-1.00** via marketable order — which happens to land in the same ballpark as this codebase's existing `min_entry_premium=0.30` floor (a plausibility check, not a validation — the floor was set from a different derivation, the noise-floor lesson). A single anecdotal SPY quote (weak confidence, one data point) suggests SPY's finer $0.01 tick could make actual round-trip cost far smaller ($0.01-0.06), which would lower this breakeven substantially — **no rigorous SPY-specific (not SPX/SPXW) 0DTE spread study exists**, this is the single highest-value external data gap. Every rigorous academic dataset found (Beckmeyer et al. n=millions, Bogousslavsky & Muravyev n=210K 0DTE trades) shows long-premium 0DTE buying — 100% of Gamma's playbook — averaging **-4.6% to -4.7% per trade**, with short/sell-side positions the only side reported profitable after fees in any of these datasets.

**Today's 2 real scalp-shaped fills (vwap_continuation, -$54/-$14) cannot be used as friction evidence either way** — both were directional-gate failures (fired counter-HTF, veto engaged only after the losses), not friction failures.

**Binding constraint, stated plainly: fixed round-trip friction (~$12/trade at qty3, the codebase's own standard) is large relative to any thin-edge scalp's per-trade dollar profit, and gets relatively worse, not better, as TP% shrinks toward 30%.** A scalp book is not ruled out — TRENDLINE_BREAK_RETEST is live-shadow-positive at a **wider, wider-hold** shape than J's ask (+$1.39/obs blended, +$4.62/obs at vol_ratio≥1.5) — but a literal 30%-TP/5-15-min shape has no supporting evidence anywhere in this research pass.

## 6. HONESTY RAILS

- **A literal 30% TP scalp shape.** Zero backtest evidence in this codebase (min TP tested = 50%), and every rigorous external 0DTE dataset shows long-premium buying losing money on average across every hold length studied. The mechanism (fixed-dollar friction vs. thinning per-trade edge) argues a 30% shape would be *worse* than the shapes already found marginal, not better.
- **Gate-loosening as a frequency fix for safe-3/risky-1.** The forensic pass's own top "biggest unlock" recommendation failed its only real P&L test (0-for-4, -$85) on adversarial re-check. Do not re-propose this without a larger, profitable sample.

Treat `evidence_n ≥ 30` as the working floor for any hypothesis whose only reason for going live was this pivot (per Deflated Sharpe Ratio reasoning, strong external confidence) — re-audit at the next quarterly gate-battery cadence (mirroring the 07-15 directional-gate battery).
